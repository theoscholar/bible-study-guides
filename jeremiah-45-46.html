Jeremiah 45-46 — Hope Is Key to Contentment

Study note · compiled Oct 05, 2026 · structure follows God’s Word Is Truth—A Handbook to the Bible · Bible reading for the week of Oct 19-25, 2026 · Treasures theme: "Hope Is Key to Contentment"

“But you are seeking great things for yourself. Stop seeking such things.” “‘For I am about to bring a calamity on all flesh,’ declares Jehovah, ‘and wherever you may go, I will grant you your life as a spoil.’”
Key text — Jeremiah 45:5 (Jehovah’s word to Baruch, 625 B.C.E.)
Focus

Two messages from the same year, 625 B.C.E. In chapter 45 Jehovah answers the private complaint of one weary secretary: stop seeking great things — I will give you your life. In chapter 46 he answers the boast of a superpower: Egypt, rising like the Nile to “cover the earth,” is broken at Carchemish, and Babylon will one day march to the Nile itself. The chapter closes with comfort for Jehovah’s own people.

Connection to the Bible’s theme

Jehovah is “the King, whose name is Jehovah of armies” (46:18). He decides the outcome of the world’s battles, and he also knows the heart of one discouraged servant. Contentment comes from trusting his purpose for the earth rather than seeking a place in a system he is about to tear down (45:4, 5).

1.Why Valuable

Chapter 45 is the shortest chapter in Jeremiah and one of the most personal in the Bible: a whole prophecy addressed to one man about his own feelings. Chapter 46 opens the book’s section of messages “concerning the nations” (46:1) with the battle that decided who would rule the Middle East. Side by side, they show the same God at two scales — and the Treasures theme for the week, “Hope Is Key to Contentment,” joins them: Baruch could stop seeking great things because Jehovah was about to do great things. The chapters reveal Jehovah’s qualities:

  • He hears the complaint we think is private. “You have said: ‘Woe to me, for Jehovah has added grief to my pain!’” (45:3). “Whichever way Baruch did it, Jehovah was listening. The Examiner of human hearts knew what caused Baruch’s troubled state, and through Jeremiah, God kindly corrected Baruch.” (jr 103 ¶2)
  • He corrects with kindness and a promise. He did not dismiss Baruch’s pain; he named the real problem — “you are seeking great things for yourself” — and in the same breath guaranteed his life (45:5).
  • He is the King over the kings. “That day belongs to the Sovereign Lord, Jehovah of armies” (46:10). Egypt’s chariots, mercenaries and boasts count for nothing when “Jehovah has pushed them down” (46:15).
  • He judges false gods. “Now I am turning my attention to Amon from No, to Pharaoh, to Egypt, to her gods” (46:25). The chief god of Egypt’s greatest city is named and judged.
  • He disciplines his people “to the proper degree.” “I will make an extermination among all the nations where I dispersed you, But you I will not exterminate. I will discipline you to the proper degree” (46:28). His justice toward his own is measured and has restoration in view.

2.What to Know Before Reading

When. Both chapters are tied to “the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, the king of Judah” (45:1; 46:2) — 625 B.C.E. Insight: “The fourth year of the reign of Jehoiakim (625 B.C.E.) saw Nebuchadnezzar defeat Pharaoh Necho in a battle over the domination of Syria-Palestine. The battle took place at Carchemish by the Euphrates, over 600 km (370 mi) N of Jerusalem. … In that same year Jeremiah began dictating to his secretary Baruch the words Jehovah directed against Israel, Judah, and all the nations” (it "Jehoiakim"). So chapter 45 belongs with chapter 36, eighteen years before Jerusalem fell, though it is placed here, after the account of the flight to Egypt that Baruch shared. The second oracle on Egypt (46:13-26) is not dated in the text.

Who was Baruch?

A man from a prominent family. “Baruch was the son of Neriah and brother of Seraiah, Zedekiah’s quartermaster who read Jeremiah’s scroll alongside the Euphrates.” (it "Baruch") Neriah was the “Son of Mahseiah, and father of both Baruch, Jeremiah’s secretary, and Seraiah, the one who read the denunciation of Babylon to that city.” (it "Neriah")

More than a scribe. Jeremiah 36:32 calls him “the secretary,” the same title borne by Elishama, who is listed among Judah’s princes (36:12). “Archaeological evidence suggests that he held the position of a high royal official.” (jr 104 ¶4) His service with Jeremiah was therefore costly: “supporting God’s prophet might have put at risk Baruch’s position and career.” (jr 105 ¶5)

Jeremiah’s closest friend. “The prophet’s closest friend appears to have been Baruch, the son of Neriah. … Baruch shared Jeremiah’s faith and conviction that what God foretold would occur.” (jr 61-62 ¶12) He wrote the scroll twice (36:4, 32), read it publicly at the temple (36:10), hid with Jeremiah from Jehoiakim (36:26), kept the deeds of the field at Anathoth (32:12-16), was blamed for the prophet’s counsel (43:3), and was taken to Egypt with him (43:6).

The outcome. “At one point during the writing of the first scroll, when Baruch complained of his weariness, Jehovah warned him: ‘Do not keep on seeking great things for yourself.’ Nevertheless, because of his faithfulness he was promised preservation and safety ‘in all the places to which he might go,’ not only during the terrible siege of Jerusalem but also afterwards when the rebellious populace compelled him and Jeremiah to go down to Egypt with them.” (it "Baruch")

The Bible does not record Baruch’s death.

Why Carchemish mattered

The place. Carchemish was “an important trade center situated on the W bank of the upper Euphrates at one of the main fords of that river.” (it "Carchemish") Whoever held it held the road between Mesopotamia and Syria-Palestine.

The battle. “In 625 B.C.E. a decisive battle was fought at Carchemish between the Egyptian and Babylonian armies. Nebuchadnezzar led the Babylonians to a smashing victory over Pharaoh Necho’s forces and swept over Syria and Canaan. This battle marked the end of Egyptian imperial strength in these regions. The Bible account at Jeremiah 46:2 is paralleled by that of the Babylonian Chronicles (B.M. 21946), both describing the defeat of the Egyptian army.” (it "Carchemish")

What it meant for Judah. Jehoiakim had been Egypt’s vassal; now Babylon was master. The Bible records the result: “Never again did the king of Egypt venture out of his land, for the king of Babylon had taken all that belonged to the king of Egypt, from the Wadi of Egypt up to the Euphrates River.” (2 Ki. 24:7) God’s Word for Us Through Jeremiah puts it simply: “King Nebuchadnezzar vanquished Pharaoh Necho’s forces, ending Egyptian power in the region.” (jr 24 ¶17)

Egypt’s army as Jeremiah describes it. Bucklers, shields, harnessed horses, helmets, polished lances and coats of mail (46:3, 4). Insight notes that among the Egyptians, “Though body armor seems to have been little used in earlier times, it later came into use as also did helmets, often plumed. Thus, Jeremiah’s prophecy … gives an accurate description of the Egyptian military in the seventh century B.C.E.” (it "Egypt, Egyptian") The foreign contingents of 46:9 are confirmed too: “At the battle of Carchemish in 625 B.C.E., Ethiopian forces formed part of Pharaoh Necho’s army, which suffered defeat there at the hands of Nebuchadnezzar.” (it "Cush") The Ludim were “noted for their proficiency with the bow” and, “together with Hamitic Put and Cush, were incorporated in Egyptian military forces.” (it "Lud, Ludim")

Writer: Jeremiah, with Baruch as his secretary. Chapter 45 preserves the scribe’s own correction in the very book he was helping to write.

What Jeremiah was doing. In 625 B.C.E. he was dictating to Baruch “all the words that Jehovah had spoken” since the days of Josiah (36:1-4), a body of messages that included the judgments on the nations that begin at 46:1. He passed on Jehovah’s correction to his friend faithfully (45:2-5): “For example, when Baruch, Jeremiah’s companion and secretary, needed vigorous counsel, Jeremiah willingly gave it.” (jr 137 ¶18)

How he coped.

  • He kept giving counsel, even to a friend. It would have been easier to let Baruch’s complaint pass. Jeremiah delivered the word as he received it, and “Baruch enjoyed God’s favor and survived Jerusalem’s destruction.” (jr 137 ¶18)
  • He kept the long view. Jeremiah could speak of Egypt’s fall years before it came (46:13), because his confidence rested on Jehovah’s word, not on how strong Egypt looked. “That must have seemed most unlikely because Egypt was so powerful, even dominating Judah.” (jr 161 ¶14)
  • He had a friend who shared his faith. The relationship ran both ways: Baruch’s company strengthened Jeremiah, and Jeremiah’s message steadied Baruch (jr 61-62 ¶12).

Background: After Josiah’s death at Megiddo, Judah was tributary to Egypt. Necho had removed Jehoahaz and installed Jehoiakim (2 Ki. 23:29-35). Babylon, having destroyed Nineveh, was contesting the remnant of Assyria and its Egyptian ally on the Euphrates. Within Judah, Jehoiakim was hostile to Jeremiah’s message; he would burn the scroll Baruch had written (36:23). For a royal official who sided with the prophet, the future at court looked bleak — the setting of Baruch’s complaint.

Other details: Chapter 46 holds two poems. The first (46:3-12) is a battle scene: the commands to prepare, the sudden rout, the Nile-like advance turned to stumbling. The second (46:14-24) is an invasion: a sword in Migdol, Noph and Tahpanhes, the “good-looking heifer” stung from the north, the forest cut down by woodcutters. Verses 25-26 sum up the judgment, and verses 27-28 repeat, almost word for word, the promise of 30:10, 11. Within this guide, citations without a book name are to Jeremiah.

3.Summary

Baruch’s complaint. (45:1-3) In 625 B.C.E., while Baruch was writing Jeremiah’s dictated words in a book, Jehovah sent him a message of his own. He quoted Baruch’s words back to him: “Woe to me, for Jehovah has added grief to my pain! I am weary from my groaning, and I have found no resting-place” (45:3).

“Stop seeking such things.” (45:4, 5) Jehovah was about to tear down what he had built up and uproot what he had planted — “the entire land” (45:4). In that setting, “you are seeking great things for yourself. Stop seeking such things.” The calamity would come “on all flesh,” but “wherever you may go, I will grant you your life as a spoil” (45:5).

Egypt is routed at Carchemish. (46:1-6) The word “concerning the nations” begins with Egypt, “concerning the army of Pharaoh Necho … defeated at Carchemish by King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon in the fourth year of Jehoiakim” (46:2). The orders ring out — shields, horses, helmets, lances, coats of mail — and then the scene breaks: “Why do I see them terror-stricken?” The swift cannot flee; “by the bank of the Euphrates River, They have stumbled and fallen” (46:5, 6).

The Nile that would cover the earth. (46:7-12) Egypt rose “like the Nile River” and boasted, “I will go up and cover the earth” (46:8), with Cush, Put and the Ludim in its ranks. But “that day belongs to the Sovereign Lord, Jehovah of armies, the day of vengeance” — a “sacrifice in the land of the north by the Euphrates River” (46:10). No balsam of Gilead could heal Egypt: “there is no cure for you” (46:11).

Nebuchadnezzar will strike Egypt. (46:13-19) A second word concerns “the coming of King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon to strike down the land of Egypt” (46:13). Migdol, Noph and Tahpanhes must prepare. Egypt’s mighty men fall because “Jehovah has pushed them down” (46:15); her hired soldiers want to go home; Pharaoh is “just a meaningless noise Who has let the opportunity pass by” (46:17). The invader will tower “like Tabor among the mountains And like Carmel by the sea” (46:18). Noph will be burned and left without an inhabitant (46:19).

Heifer, serpent and forest. (46:20-24) Egypt is “a good-looking heifer,” but “stinging flies will come against her from the north” (46:20). Her mercenaries, “like fattened calves,” flee (46:21). Her sound is “like that of a slithering serpent” as enemies come “with axes, Like men cutting down trees” (46:22, 23). “The daughter of Egypt … will be handed over to the people of the north” (46:24).

Judgment on Amon, hope for Egypt — and for Jacob. (46:25-28) Jehovah turns his attention “to Amon from No, to Pharaoh, to Egypt, to her gods, and to her kings” and hands them to Nebuchadnezzar; “but afterward she will be inhabited as in times past” (46:25, 26). To his own people he says: “Do not be afraid, my servant Jacob … For I will save you from far away” (46:27). He will discipline them “to the proper degree,” but he will never exterminate them (46:28).

4.Benefit From Jeremiah 45-46

1. Draw closer to Jehovah

  1. Jehovah quoted Baruch’s own words back to him (45:3). What does it tell you about Jehovah that he heard a complaint Baruch may have voiced only in his heart? (jr 103 ¶2)
  2. Jehovah corrected Baruch and in the same message promised to preserve his life (45:5). How does that combination draw you to Jehovah when you need correction? (it "Baruch")
  3. “The King, whose name is Jehovah of armies” (46:18) decided the battle of Carchemish and judged Amon of No. How does seeing his control over the nations help you trust him with your own future? (46:10, 25; jr 161 ¶14)
  4. “I will discipline you to the proper degree” (46:28). How does that phrase reassure you about the way Jehovah deals with his people? (jr 139 ¶20)

2. Deepen your understanding

The question: Jer 46:22 — In what way would the sound of Egypt become “like that of a slithering serpent”?

Insight explains the figure under “Voice”: “Through the prophet Jeremiah, God also prophesied that Egypt would be vanquished by the Babylonians, who would come in force as woodcutters, to chop her down. She would lie on the ground, deeply humbled, weeping softly and moaning, her “voice” being low like that of a hissing serpent in retreat.” (it "Voice" ¶16)

The point: Egypt had come up roaring like the Nile in flood, boasting that it would “cover the earth” (46:8). Its end would be the opposite — not a roar but a hiss, the low sound of a snake slipping away as the axes ring through its “forest” (46:22, 23). Pharaoh, who had made so much noise, would be remembered as “just a meaningless noise” (46:17).

The heifer and the stinging flies. “The prophet Jeremiah spoke figuratively of the nation of Egypt, when settled prosperously and well fed in her land, as “a very pretty heifer” but foretold that her defeat was to come.” (it "Heifer") The pests “from the north” (46:20) are the Babylonian armies.

Tabor and Carmel. Neither mountain is especially high, but both stand out. Jehovah “used the striking massiveness of Tabor—standing alone in the Valley of Jezreel—to illustrate the impressiveness of Nebuchadnezzar upon his entering Egypt with a mighty military force.” (it "Tabor") Likewise, “The majestic appearance of Carmel, particularly the headland that sweeps dramatically upward from the coast, even as Mount Tabor rises impressively in the Valley of Jezreel, was also used to represent the imposing figure of Nebuchadnezzar advancing to the conquest of Egypt.” (it "Carmel")

No balsam for Egypt. The healing resin of Gilead was famous. “All references to such healing properties in the Scriptures are made by Jeremiah. He uses these, however, in a figurative sense, first when lamenting the spiritual breakdown in Judah …, then in chiding Egypt as to her vain efforts to avoid defeat by Babylon …, and finally in pronouncing God’s judgment of calamity against Babylon.” (it "Balsam, Balsam of Gilead")

Amon from No. “At Thebes (Biblical No) the god Amon was most prominent and in time was accorded the title “king of the gods” under the name Amon-Ra.” (it "Egypt, Egyptian") By naming Amon, Jehovah was naming the god Egypt held highest. “Today only small villages are to be found around the massive ruins of the temples of the impotent gods of No.” (it "No, No-Amon")

3. Apply in your life

Imitate Jehovah’s qualities.

  • Listen for what lies behind a complaint. Jehovah heard Baruch’s “woe” and answered the cause, not just the words (45:3-5). When a friend is discouraged, we can listen long enough to understand what is really troubling him.
  • Pair correction with hope. Jehovah’s counsel to Baruch ended in a promise (45:5). When we must give counsel, we can do the same, showing the person what he will gain by accepting it.
  • Discipline in measure. “To the proper degree” (46:28). Parents and elders can imitate Jehovah by fitting discipline to the situation and keeping restoration in view (jr 139 ¶20).

Other lessons.

  • Discontent can creep in while we are busy serving. “The prophet Jeremiah’s secretary, Baruch, temporarily fell into a trap. Baruch had a difficult assignment, namely, to support Jeremiah as he delivered a strong message to an ungrateful nation. At one point, Baruch lost his focus. Instead of concentrating on what Jehovah wanted him to do, he apparently began thinking too much about himself and what he wanted to do.” (w25.07 22 ¶8) In effect, Jehovah was saying: “Be content with your present circumstances.” And “Baruch accepted the correction and continued to enjoy Jehovah’s approval.” (w25.07 22 ¶8)
  • Privileges are gifts, not wages. “Every privilege we receive and every talent we have comes from Jehovah. We do not earn or deserve these gifts. They are all expressions of Jehovah’s undeserved kindness.” (w25.07 23 ¶9)
  • Discontent is dangerous. “Others have become so disappointed at not receiving a privilege of service that they have given up serving Jehovah altogether. … The problem may be that the person has stopped cultivating contentment.” (w25.07 20 ¶2)
  • Hope restores focus. Jehovah turned Baruch’s eyes to what was coming: a calamity on all flesh, and his own survival (45:5). “Now more than ever, we need to focus on our hope.” (w25.07 24 ¶13) “If we are anxious about privileges of service that we have not yet received, we can think about how insignificant such anxieties will be after we reach perfection and have served Jehovah for thousands of years.” (w25.07 24 ¶14)
  • Examine the heart, and the hopes we hold for our children. “Ask yourself, ‘Do I have “ambitions,” maybe ones securely guarded in my heart, to make a success of a worldly career now or in the near future?’” (jr 108 ¶11) And for parents: “As Baruch might have been tempted to seek prominence via his position or career, a parent might inwardly seek such through the achievements of his offspring.” (jr 109 ¶13)
  • Life is the real prize. “We need to remember that what Jehovah has promised as a spoil is our “soul.”” (jr 112 ¶18) “Some may find it sobering to think that they might survive with only their “soul” as a spoil, but that actually should be no disappointment at all.” (jr 112 ¶19)
  • Do not trust the Nile. Egypt looked unstoppable and was broken in a single battle (46:7-12). The powers of this world can impress us just as much; the contrast between Egypt’s boast and its end helps us keep them in proportion.

Stop seeking such things.”’ “‘For I am about to bring a calamity on all flesh,’ declares Jehovah, ‘and wherever you may go, I will grant you your life as a spoil.’””

Jeremiah 46:2

“For Egypt, concerning the army of Pharaoh Necho the king of Egypt, who was along the Euphrates River and was defeated at Carchemish by King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, the king of Judah:”

Jeremiah 46:5-6

“‘Why do I see them terror-stricken? They are retreating, their warriors are crushed. They have fled in panic, their warriors have not turned around. There is terror all around,’ declares Jehovah. ‘The swift cannot flee, and the warriors cannot escape. In the north, by the bank of the Euphrates River, They have stumbled and fallen.’”

Jeremiah 46:8-10

“Egypt comes up just like the Nile River, Like rivers of surging waters, And it says, ‘I will go up and cover the earth. I will destroy the city and those inhabiting it.’ Go up, you horses! Drive madly, you chariots! Let the warriors advance, Cush and Put, who handle the shield, And the Ludim, who handle and bend the bow. “That day belongs to the Sovereign Lord, Jehovah of armies, the day of vengeance for taking revenge on his adversaries. And the sword will devour and satisfy itself and take its fill of their blood, for the Sovereign Lord, Jehovah of armies, has a sacrifice in the land of the north by the Euphrates River.”

Jeremiah 46:13-15

“This is the word that Jehovah spoke to Jeremiah the prophet regarding the coming of King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon to strike down the land of Egypt: “Declare it in Egypt, proclaim it in Migdol. Proclaim it in Noph and in Tahpanhes. Say, ‘Take your positions and prepare yourselves, For a sword will devour all around you. Why have your powerful men been swept away? They did not stand their ground, For Jehovah has pushed them down.”

Jeremiah 46:17-18

“There they have proclaimed, ‘Pharaoh king of Egypt is just a meaningless noise Who has let the opportunity pass by.’ ‘As surely as I am alive,’ declares the King, whose name is Jehovah of armies, ‘He will come in like Tabor among the mountains And like Carmel by the sea.”

Jeremiah 46:20-22

“Egypt is like a good-looking heifer, But stinging flies will come against her from the north. Even her hired soldiers in her midst are like fattened calves, But they have also turned back and fled together. They could not stand their ground, For the day of their disaster has come upon them, Their time of reckoning.’ ‘Her sound is like that of a slithering serpent, For they come after her in force, with axes, Like men cutting down trees.”

Jeremiah 46:25-26

““Jehovah of armies, the God of Israel, says: ‘Now I am turning my attention to Amon from No, to Pharaoh, to Egypt, to her gods, and to her kings—yes, to Pharaoh and all those trusting in him.’ “‘And I will hand them over to those seeking to take their life, to King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon and his servants. But afterward she will be inhabited as in times past,’ declares Jehovah.”

Jeremiah 46:27-28

“‘As for you, do not be afraid, my servant Jacob, And do not be terrified, O Israel. For I will save you from far away And your offspring from the land of their captivity. Jacob will return and be calm and undisturbed, With no one to make them afraid. So do not be afraid, my servant Jacob,’ declares Jehovah, ‘for I am with you. I will make an extermination among all the nations where I dispersed you, But you I will not exterminate. I will discipline you to the proper degree, But I will by no means leave you unpunished.’””
